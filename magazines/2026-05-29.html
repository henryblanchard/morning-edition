Your Car Has Been Talking About You

Modern passenger cars collect location history, call logs, contact lists, biometric stress markers, driving style data, and voice recordings, much of it transmitted continuously to manufacturers and third-party data brokers under consent buried in hundred-page terms no one reads. The BBC Future investigation is quietly alarming: not because the data collection is new, but because the scale and the downstream markets for that data are accelerating. The Hands Know Before the Words Do

Researchers at the Max Planck Institute for Psycholinguistics found that Italian and Dutch teachers, from radically different gesture cultures, use nearly identical hand movements when conveying spatial concepts to young learners. The finding suggests some pedagogical gestures may be more universal than culturally learned. For anyone who teaches with their hands as well as their voice, it is a practical and somewhat reassuring result: there may be a shared physical grammar of explanation underneath the linguistic differences.

One Flower, One House. The Maths of Mania.

The Dutch tulip bubble of 1637 is the canonical speculative collapse, cited at the top of every crypto post-mortem and repeated in every finance course. This Dutch Review piece tells it with unusual clarity: single bulbs trading for the price of a house on a single market day, a collapse within a week, and a moral tale that became canonical precisely because it was so hard to explain as rational. Every new bubble looks different from the inside.
