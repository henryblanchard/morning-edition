Database

The SQLite Setting Most Developers Don't Know Exists

STRICT mode tables arrived in SQLite 3.37 and have been quietly available ever since. They enforce actual column types at insertion rather than relying on SQLite's famously permissive type affinity rules, which silently accept almost anything. The author makes an unambiguous case: use strict tables unless you have a specific reason not to. It is the small irreversible decision that saves you from an entire category of bugs you would never have anticipated finding.

What If Everyone's Laptop Was the Server?

Iroh is a networking protocol for building peer-to-peer applications. Mesh LLM is the experiment built on top of it: distribute an AI model across several consumer devices, run inference collectively, own no central infrastructure. The project is early and throughput is modest. The concept is significant. As compute centralises ever further around hyperscalers, this asks whether the inverse is architecturally possible, and what AI access might look like if it were.

India's Payment Network Moves Billions a Month. Here's How.

UPI processes several billion transactions monthly across a country of 1.4 billion people. For anyone interested in financial infrastructure, payment product design, or the policy choices required to build at national scale, this is a clear and methodical dissection of something most engineers see daily and almost none understand.

What Doctors Actually Choose When They Become the Patient

When physicians receive a terminal diagnosis, they overwhelmingly choose less intervention than they prescribe for others. No ventilators. No heroic surgeries. Comfort care at home. This essay, published in 2011 and resurfacing with good reason, asks a question that quietly haunts medicine: if the people who best understand the system systematically opt out of it at the end, what does that tell us about what the system is actually for?

Science

Long COVID May Be a Nerve Problem in the Stomach

New research finds that Long COVID patients show degradation of the vagal cholinergic fibres in the gastric mucosa: the neural wiring connecting gut to brain. The vagus nerve governs heart rate, digestion, inflammation, and mood. Peripheral damage here would explain Long COVID's most baffling symptom cluster: fatigue, gut dysfunction, and cognitive fog arriving together, without requiring direct injury to the brain itself. The mechanism is precise. The implications for treatment are significant.

Physics

We Thought We Knew How Liquids Work

New research reported by Quanta shows that simple fluids, under specific conditions, can fracture like a solid rather than flow around an obstacle. This challenges a foundational assumption of fluid mechanics: that liquids, by definition, cannot sustain cracks. The conditions required are precise and extreme. The principle itself is disorienting. Some things we treated as definitional turn out to have exceptions. That is, roughly speaking, how physics has always advanced.

What Fifty Million Drawings Reveal About the Way Cultures See

Researchers analysed over 50 million sketches from Google's Quick Draw dataset across more than 100 countries. The results show systematic cross-cultural variation in how people represent common concepts: a clock drawn in Japan looks structurally different from one drawn in Germany. The variations cluster by region, language family, and geography. What we assume are universal mental representations may be culturally learned, drawn in the mind before they are drawn on the page.
