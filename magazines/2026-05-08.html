Ultrasound shakes the virus to pieces.

A Brazilian-led team report that frequencies tuned to the resonant signature of viral capsids appear to fracture influenza A and SARS-CoV-2 particles in a dish, while leaving human cells intact. The mechanism is acoustic resonance, not heat. If the result holds beyond the bench, it points at a class of physical disinfection methods that need no new chemistry.

The interesting bit

Each virus has a structural frequency at which it vibrates apart, like a wine glass under a sustained note. The team modelled the capsids first, picked the frequency from the model, and then watched the particles disassemble in real time on the imaging stage.

Twenty-five volunteers  ·  two extra virtual limbs  ·  cortical remapping

Twenty-five volunteers were taught to fly with two extra virtual limbs in a controlled VR rig. After enough hours of training, motor-cortex recordings showed the brain encoding the new wings less like tools and more like real, owned body parts. The result extends the body-schema literature past prosthetics and pokes at how flexible the inventory of "what counts as me" actually is.
