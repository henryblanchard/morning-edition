John C. Dvorak, 1946–2026. Tech journalism's most reliable contrarian.

Dvorak was wrong about many things: he predicted the Mac would fail, that the internet was a fad, that podcasting wouldn't last. He was also, often enough to matter, exactly right. The Medici mystery that took 400 years to close

Several members of the Medici family died in ways that never made sense. The historical record blamed poisoning, political murder, or bad luck. Researchers now believe the answer is malaria, specifically a particularly virulent strain of the parasite common in 15th and 16th century Tuscany.

The evidence comes from DNA extracted from the family's remains in the Basilica di San Lorenzo. This is forensic genomics doing something historians alone could not: reopening a cold case that had been cold for four centuries and arriving at a plausible answer.

The Medici funded the Renaissance. Their deaths shaped Italian politics for generations. The cause, it turns out, was a mosquito.
