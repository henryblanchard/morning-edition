Story Six  ·  Mathematics
T

ao Digests a 50-Year-Old Conjecture's Collapse

Terence Tao has published a careful walkthrough of a recent counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture, one of mathematics' most persistently famous open problems in algebraic geometry. The conjecture, which posited that certain polynomial maps from real space to itself must be globally invertible if their derivative never vanishes, has stood since 1939. Tao's note is not the proof itself but something arguably more useful: a lucid unpacking of what the counterexample actually achieves, and what it does not.
