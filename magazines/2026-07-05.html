Better Models. Worse Tools.

Armin Ronacher argues that as language models have improved dramatically, the tooling built around them has quietly regressed. Autocomplete breaks when it knows too much. IDE integrations overreach. The interface layer is struggling to keep up with what the model can actually do, and that gap is the silent tax every developer pays. Required reading for anyone building or teaching with AI: the bottleneck is no longer the model.

Agent Architecture · arXiv

The Audit Trail Should Be the Agent Itself

An arXiv paper argues that in agentic AI systems, the execution log should not be a side record of what happened: it should be the primary artefact that defines and constrains what the agent can do next. The distinction sounds subtle until you sit with it. If the log is the agent, then every action is self-documenting, every state transition is auditable, and the system can be paused, rewound, or handed to a human at any point without loss of context. If you are building anything with autonomous agents, this framing is worth the thirty minutes.

Weird Science

A Creature With No Brain Heals Faster Than Anything With One

Researchers at the Marine Biological Laboratory found that jellyfish can close wounds in minutes by reorganising their muscle structure. No nervous system coordinates the repair, no inflammation signal, no clotting cascade: just immediate, distributed self-repair at the cellular level. Scientists believe the mechanism has no known parallel in vertebrate biology. Whether it can be borrowed remains an open question and, probably, a very lucrative one.

Astronomy

European Southern Observatory

We Are Paving Over the Night Sky

The ESO has published an unusually direct assessment: proliferating satellite constellations and proposed orbital mirrors are pushing light pollution beyond any threshold that professional astronomy can work around. The night sky, once treated as a commons, is now a congested commercial zone. The damage, the paper suggests, may prove difficult to reverse. This is not a forecast. The change is already measurable from ground-based observatories on four continents.

Programming

Just learn SQL.

A 2014 essay by Kamil Wozniak that keeps resurfacing on Hacker News: 155 points and 189 comments this week. The argument is simple and has not aged. Every ORM eventually collapses under real-world query complexity, and by the time it does, you have forgotten the SQL that would have solved the problem in ten minutes. Twelve years on, the situation has not changed. If anything, the AI tooling era has made it worse: models generate SQL fluently, but only if you can tell them what you actually want.
