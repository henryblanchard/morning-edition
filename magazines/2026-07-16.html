A browser inside a browser

Someone compiled the entire Firefox browser -- Gecko, SpiderMonkey, the full UI stack -- to WebAssembly and ran it inside an HTML canvas element. The JIT alone cost over $25,000 in AI compute tokens to debug. The result is strange, brilliant, and a demonstration that WebAssembly's ceiling is considerably higher than almost anyone imagined.

The case for an AI commons

A Siegel Endowment policy brief argues that concentrating AI capability in a handful of private labs is a structural risk, and that governments, companies, and nonprofits should fund open alternatives the same way they fund roads and universities. A quiet but important argument about who controls the infrastructure of the next decade.

Weird Science

Cagesmade of almost nothing

Metal-organic frameworks are porous materials with internal surface areas so vast that a single gram of one can hold the equivalent area of a football pitch. Originally a curiosity from 2018, they are now being used for carbon capture, drug delivery, and harvesting drinking water from desert air. This piece from Berkeley is worth revisiting; the field has moved fast.

Thirteen hundred animals, restored

Open Culture has gathered and published 1,300 wildlife illustrations from 19th-century natural history publications, all digitally restored from their original plates. They are strikingly beautiful. They are also a reminder that scientific illustration was once a serious art form with real stakes, practiced by people who had never seen a photograph and had to get it right the first time.
