Google's Antigravity uses an AI /teamwork command to accelerate science and engineering.
Google Research published a report on Antigravity's /teamwork feature, an agentic workflow that lets researchers invoke collaborative AI assistance mid-task. The framing is deliberate: rather than AI as a solo assistant, Antigravity positions it as a team member that joins ongoing work. For educators designing AI-assisted curricula, the model is worth studying. The jump in productivity comes not from replacing the researcher but from lowering the cost of the first draft, the literature scan, and the hypothesis test. Wherever a workflow has a bottleneck between knowing what to try and trying it, /teamwork shrinks the gap.

Running an AI prompt in Spanish or Hindi costs measurably more energy than in English.

For anyone building AI-powered tools for global audiences, or teaching non-English speakers to use AI, this is a structural inequality baked into current model architectures that no prompt-engineering fix will solve.
