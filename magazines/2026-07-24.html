Writing by Hand Is Good for Your Brain

Neal Stephenson, the novelist who coined "metaverse", argues that handwriting bypasses the shortcuts our brains take when we type. It is slower, more deliberate, and that is precisely the point. The friction is the feature: the pen forces a kind of cognitive presence that a keyboard actively discourages. In an era of voice-to-text and autocomplete, this is a mild but genuine act of resistance.

What Just Happened to TheNumbers.com Should Worry All of Us

For decades, TheNumbers.com was the authoritative source for box office statistics: deep, reliable, specialist knowledge accumulated over many years. Then AI scrapers consumed it wholesale, redistributed the content across summary pages and chatbot outputs, and killed its search ranking in the process. Stephen Follows documents the collapse in forensic detail. The story is not about one website. It is about what happens when the aggregate value of a field's accumulated expertise is hoovered up, repackaged, and returned to the field as a competitor.

Echo: Fable-Level Performance at a Third of the Cost, Using Open-Weight Models

Echo routes each prompt through a curated pool of open-weight models, deciding in real time which combinations of GLM, Kimi K2, and others to deploy, and how to weight their outputs. The system was built on a single observation: the gap between "the best single model" and "the best possible routing" is where the real efficiency gains live. On standard evaluations, the combined system outperforms any individual model in the pool. The claim of Fable-level results at roughly a third of the cost is a strong one; the architecture is transparent enough to interrogate it properly.

Why Software Factories Fail, and Why Harness Engineering Is Not Enough

The "context engineering" framing for AI coding agents is everywhere right now. But this essay argues that better scaffolding is not the binding constraint. The deeper problem is that software factories require consistent, composable, machine-readable specifications; and most organisations do not have them, have never needed them, and cannot retrofit them in a sprint. More harness will not fix that. The post is long and occasionally combative. It is worth reading before you commission another AI coding workflow.

"Harness engineering is necessary but not sufficient. The missing ingredient is specification discipline."

Astronomers May Have Found the First Moon Orbiting a Planet Beyond Our Solar System

Using ESO's Very Large Telescope, a team has identified what may be a genuine exomoon: a natural satellite orbiting a gas giant roughly 150 light years from Earth. If confirmed, it would be the first moon ever detected outside our solar system. The candidate object appears to be large, perhaps Neptune-sized, and the confirmation process will likely take years. Still. The first exomoon. That is a sentence worth sitting with for a moment.
