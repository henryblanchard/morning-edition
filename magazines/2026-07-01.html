Neuroscience

Meta taught a computer to read your thoughts. No surgery required.

Meta's brain2qwerty research team has demonstrated a system that decodes intended keystrokes from scalp-mounted EEG sensors, with no implant required. A participant wearing the headset could type words at useful accuracy purely through the intent to move their fingers. This is meaningfully different from prior brain-computer interface work, which typically required electrode arrays implanted directly in the motor cortex. The non-invasive route has always been considered the harder problem; this suggests it may be more tractable than assumed.

seven.

The machine that found the Higgs boson goes dark

CERN has powered down the Large Hadron Collider and entered Long Shutdown 3, a multi-year upgrade programme before the machine restarts at higher energies. The LHC confirmed the Higgs boson in 2012, closed a fifty-year gap in the Standard Model, and has operated without catastrophic failure ever since. It will return, upgraded. For now, a particular kind of physics goes quiet. The silence has its own weight.

Retro Technology

Knoppix: the Linux that needed no hard drive, and still doesn't

Knoppix was the live Linux distribution that ran entirely from a bootable CD, no installation required, and became one of the most downloaded pieces of software of the early 2000s. You could hand someone a disc, tell them there was another operating system, and watch it boot on whatever laptop was nearby. The project is still active, now at version 9.1.

The HN thread is a reminder of what it felt like when the concept of a live operating system was genuinely new, and the comment section is full of people who remember exactly where they first booted it. Some technical things earn their nostalgia. This is one of them.
