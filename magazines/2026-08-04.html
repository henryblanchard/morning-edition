The model rewards the already-expert

Sean Goedecke makes an observation that cuts to the heart of AI's equity problem: LLMs give more useful answers to people who already know enough to ask good questions. For those who don't, the output feels impressive until it leads them somewhere wrong. AI amplifies existing knowledge gaps as readily as it closes them. Essential reading for anyone teaching AI literacy.

The case for typing the code yourself

Ankur Sethi argues that copying AI-generated code without carefully reading it creates cognitive debt: you ship something that works but that you do not understand, which compounds into systems nobody can maintain. The counter-intuitive fix is to retype it manually. Slowing down for comprehension is not inefficiency. It is the actual work of learning, even in 2026, and it applies just as directly to anyone teaching others to work with AI tools.

Why does the obvious take centuries to arrive?
